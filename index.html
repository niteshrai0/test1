I ENTERED THROUGH THE INTEGRATION LAYER

Every insurer spoke a different language. The repetition became the insight.

Different APIs, schemas, authentication, field names, validations, product rules, error behaviour, dependencies and exceptions made every integration look unique. Working across roughly 60+ insurer/product integrations early on helped me see the recurring structure underneath.

BESPOKE→60+ EXPOSURES→PATTERNS→STANDARD STRUCTURE→REPEATABLE FRAMEWORK
WHAT I CHANGED

Standardised how integrations were understood and documented across workflow/API sequence, request-response structures, field definitions, mandatory/optional attributes, mappings, validations, boundary conditions, errors, assumptions and dependencies—turning repeated ambiguity into a more structured integration playbook.

MOVING UP THE STACK

Once I understood the interfaces, I could see the product they were enabling.

My scope expanded from integration standardisation into the configurable rules and enterprise workflows that let the same underlying platform behave differently for different lenders, products and insurer combinations.

PRODUCT LENS

The recurring question became: which differences are genuinely unique, and which should become configuration, rules or reusable capabilities instead of another one-off implementation?

INTELLIGENCE INSIDE THE SAME SYSTEM

The workflow knew how to execute. Could it also help someone decide?

AIRe addressed a real decision point inside the assisted insurance journey: matching customer and loan context against product information, eligibility and business constraints. The product evolved from a single recommendation toward a ranked shortlist while keeping the salesperson in control of the customer decision.

Customer context+Loan context+Product context+Constraints→ AIRe →Ranked recommendation→Human decision
MY CONTRIBUTION

Problem definition, decision context, recommendation workflow, requirements, business constraints, experience design, engineering collaboration, validation and iteration. AI should serve the decision, not replace the person.

FROM INTEGRATION PROBLEM → PRODUCT LIFECYCLE

My scope increasingly touched the journey the infrastructure existed to make possible.

Borrower/co-borrower flows, pricing, proposal, consent, payment, insurer orchestration, issuance and enterprise workflows became part of a much broader product surface I worked across.

HOW I WORKED

Discovery → requirements → workflow/UX → engineering & QA → UAT → launch → partner onboarding → insurer coordination → analytics → iteration. The integration layer was how I entered deeply; broader LendPro ownership was what followed.

THE PRODUCT KEPT EXPANDING

Issuance wasn't the end of the workflow.

Claims, cancellations, renewals, dashboards, bulk operations, reporting, servicing, cross-sell/upsell and operational exceptions extended LendPro from a sales-oriented journey toward fuller lifecycle infrastructure.

BUSINESS CONTEXT, NOT PERSONAL ATTRIBUTION

I worked on LendPro as it operated at $2M+ ARR product/business scale. I treat that as the scale of the product I helped evolve—not revenue I personally generated.

02 / THE ARC

Every product took me
one layer deeper.

I started with technology. Product made me curious about users and businesses. Building across very different products kept revealing another layer underneath. The deeper I went, the more the questions changed.

01
TECHNOLOGY

How does it work?

Computer Science and AI/ML gave me the foundation to understand how software, data and systems are built. But knowing how to build something raised a more important question: what is worth building?

02
PRODUCT

What should we build — and why?

As an APM at Awign, I worked across enterprise requirements, configuration, mobile and web experiences, user problems, prioritisation, engineering delivery, feedback and iteration. Shipping wasn't the outcome. The product had to make something in the real world work better.

03
BUSINESS

What actually creates the value?

Working close to users, clients and operations exposed everything around the software—acquisition, onboarding, training, incentives, execution, support, payments and retention. The question changed from “does the feature work?” to “does the whole system create the intended outcome?”

04
WORKFLOWS

What actually has to happen for that outcome to exist?

Across very different products, I kept finding people, information, decisions, actions and state changes coordinated toward an outcome. Different industries and users; underneath, both were orchestrating workflows.

AWIGN · Configure → Qualify → Allocate → Execute → QC → PayRISKCOVRY · Context → Eligibility → Recommend → Consent → Issue → Service → Reconcile
05
SYSTEMS

Why do some of the simplest products have so much complexity underneath?

WHAT THE SYSTEM HANDLES

I started seeing simplicity differently—not as the absence of complexity, but as complexity successfully absorbed by the system.
06
PLATFORMS

What shouldn't we have to solve again?

Repeated complexity became easier to notice. Awign turned different enterprise requirements into configurable workflow primitives. Riskcovry turned heterogeneous partners and providers into more standardised interfaces and orchestration. Which differences are truly unique—and which should become reusable capabilities?

07
INFRASTRUCTURE

What if the reusable capability becomes the product?

Sometimes the most interesting product isn't the destination a customer visits. It's the layer that enables another product to offer a capability. Insurance gave me one implementation; I started seeing related architectures in payments, healthtech, rewards, embedded finance and contextual products.

AGGREGATE→NORMALISE→CONFIGURE→ORCHESTRATE→EXPOSE→EMBED
08
INTELLIGENCE

What happens when the system can help decide — not only execute?

AIRe introduced another layer to the same thinking. Once a system understands context, products and constraints, intelligence can help determine what should happen next—not replacing the workflow, but becoming another primitive inside it.

THE LOOP AROUND EVERY LAYER

Products are never finished.

OBSERVE→UNDERSTAND→HYPOTHESIZE→BUILD→SHIP→MEASURE→LEARN→EVOLVE ↺

Users change. Markets move. Technology improves. Regulations evolve. Edge cases appear. Assumptions fail. The product at any moment is only the latest version of what we've learned.

STILL EXPLORING

The questions keep getting deeper.

I started by wanting to understand how technology works. Building products made me curious about what creates value. That led me into workflows, the systems underneath them, the platforms that make them reusable, and the infrastructure other products can build upon. Now AI is changing what those systems can understand and decide.
